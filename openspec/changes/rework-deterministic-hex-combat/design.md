# Design

## Context

See proposal.md for motivation and the six capability deltas for the behavior contract. `CombatSimulation` owns the Arch world and currently reads sorted stable identities, moves continuous circular bodies with swept constraints/local tangents, then starts/resolves attacks. `WeaponProfile` mixes double range/speed with integer windup/cadence; `Catalogs` hardcodes some specialist/tower timings. `Match.Step` handles elimination, redistribution and wave results after combat and advances ticks only in Combat. Damage already uses integer hundredths and a common simultaneous accumulator.

`CombatPlayback` buffers complete snapshots and events at one bounded tick clock, with geometry-based interpolation safety. `UnitView` samples imported attack markers but starts death from a local event time and expires visuals after 1.4 presentation seconds. Current snapshots omit removed casualties; reconnect explicitly suppresses corpses. DevRunner's contact checks assume a 12-by-3.4 continuous corridor. Those assumptions must be replaced together, not kept as a second numerical model.

The source protocol constant is currently 5; README still describes v4. There is no persistent match-save format to migrate. Camera controls are now archived and countryside placement is still being implemented; use their current/final framing and placement interfaces. The countryside design explicitly keeps combat unsnapped; this change supersedes that combat-only assumption without altering its scenery generation or camera controls.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** One numerical owner for live units and retained death reservations; integer spatial/action rules; explainable decisions and reproducible traces; independently tunable profiles; bounded computation and battle lifetime; complete reconstruction from ordinary snapshots.

**Non-Goals:** General navigation/physics, terrain obstacles inferred from models, cover/line-of-sight, ranged kiting, simultaneous attack-and-move actions, intra-hex combat or player orders, a replay browser, save/restart recovery, runtime balance editing or new assets/dependencies. The seed/configuration contract enables repeatable fixtures and diagnostics; it does not promise replay compatibility across rules versions.

## Decisions

### 1. Model a bounded hex graph with capacity footprints

Add numerical board/configuration definitions under `src/Game.Core/Combat`. Use integer axial coordinates, canonical cell IDs, sorted six-neighbor lists, designated entry bands and a virtual city-defense anchor outside unit capacity. Hex distance is shortest-path distance on the declared cell graph. The virtual anchor has declared distance links to legal siege-frontier cells and is used only as a distance endpoint, never as a movement node or a shortcut between cells. Precompute static distances and valid footprint transitions once per frozen configuration; never infer neighbors from rendered geometry.

Start with a configurable three-column, seven-row combat patch corresponding to countryside columns -1..1 and rows -5..1, separate from building rows 2..4. This is 21 combat cells, with six positions per cell. Each end has a permanently protected rear deployment row and a neutral forward formation row. Board dimensions/spacing are provisional until the early gameplay/visual gate; the specified spread/rear/fallback formation policy is part of the contract. The defense anchor links directly to the neutral home-side siege frontier, so exposed-city melee remains possible without entering protected allied cells. Towers all use that distance origin regardless of building slot.

Positions have stable indices. Legal footprint templates for capacity costs 1, 2, 3 and 6 consume that many positions; cost-two and cost-three templates use authored neighboring position groups. Different footprints may leave fragmentation even when total free capacity looks sufficient. Admission requires an actual free legal footprint, never just a free-count comparison. A footprint's graphical anchor is declared separately. The type attribute is `CapacityCost`; the homogeneous maximum is floor(hex capacity / cost), subject to footprint compatibility. There is no conflicting type-specific head-count rule.

Maintain faction ownership over the union of occupied and reserved positions in each cell. Dying bodies keep that ownership until expiry. Stationary allies can share unused capacity; opponents cannot enter even if a cell has spare positions. Use the protected deployment and explicit formation policy below, lower initiative within a unit placement tier and seeded actor ties. Cell and footprint preference order is declared independently of target randomness. Do not create a unit twice while it waits off-board.

Alternative: one unit per hex is simpler but contradicts the confirmed shared-hex requirement. Anonymous capacity totals cannot guarantee distinct places or legal mixed footprints. Sub-position distance/physics would reintroduce the geometry problem.

### 1.1. Guarantee reinforcement admission with protected deployment cells

Give each cell immutable deployment affinity: Adventurers, Skeletons or neutral. On the candidate board, allied rear row 1 and enemy rear row -5 are protected for their respective faction; rows 0 and -4 are neutral forward formation bands. Opponents can never start, end, transit through or reserve a move in the other faction's protected row, including after that battlefield clears. An owner may occupy, move out of and fight from its protected cells. Protection restricts occupation, not damage: deployed occupants have ordinary health/range/timers and can be attacked from adjacent neutral cells or by ranged/defense attacks. There is no immunity or invulnerability window.

At wave setup, plan rear support placement first so melee overflow cannot consume its positions, then forward melee placement; activate the completed formation together. Within each tier order actors by initiative and seeded exact ties. For each actor choose a legal cell in its tier with the smallest used/reserved capacity, then the declared owner-relative column preference (center, left, right on the candidate board). Choose its forwardmost compatible footprint, then declared footprint index. Thus melee spreads across frontage before packing another unit in a hex; support starts in the rear and does not fill spare front cells merely because they are empty. Overflow queues after assigned tier capacity is exhausted. Rear reservation here means allocation during setup, not a permanent class ownership rule.

During combat, queued melee tries free legal forward-band footprints, then the protected rear fallback; queued support uses the protected rear. Order queued actors by melee/support tier, initiative and persistent seeded scheduling rank. Admission shares the existing atomic occupancy/transit rules and never bypasses faction affinity. If front cells are occupied by surviving defenders when enemies transfer in, protected enemy rear cells remain a guaranteed fallback. A cleared city's enemy rear can contain only empty positions or unexpired enemy deaths: no living enemy remains and defenders cannot enter it. After the last relevant death releases, the first fitting reinforcement is admitted in that same admission phase, or immediately in the transfer tick if a footprint is already free. Queued enemies are still counted during that bounded delay, so it cannot look like another wave clear.

Publish the predicted first-admission bound in diagnostics and verify it independently. At transfer, inspect the actual protected footprint masks for the queued profiles: the bound is the current tick when any fits, otherwise the earliest existing death-end tick at which releasing the due bodies' complete reservations permits at least one to fit. Evaluate cumulative releases in tick order, not the first corpse expiry or free-capacity totals; fragmentation can require several releases. The bound cannot exceed the latest relevant retained death deadline, because the cleared protected row contains no living enemies and every allowed profile must fit there when empty. Retain the original bound in diagnostics rather than advancing it on retries. This guarantees the first admission, not simultaneous admission of the entire allocation; subsequent overflow still follows ordinary queue, combat and death rules. The board validator also requires each protected row to connect to usable attack positions through its own frontier. With finite default armies and positive ordinary damage, already deployed reinforcements can engage and deaths allow remaining queues to progress. Conservation alone is insufficient acceptance: the cleared-board transfer fixture must observe actual admission and a subsequent attack/health change well before the configured stall allowance. `BattleStalled` remains a diagnostic bound for exceptional failures, not the entrance-clearing rule.

During pursuit there are no permanent column/lane assignments. Keep closest-target and shortest-approach ranking. Among equal shortest goal footprints, prefer one screened by a living friendly melee hex on a shortest path to the selected opponent for ranged/magic units, then the least used-capacity goal cell, then seeded ties. This preference never overrides a closer target, shorter route, in-range holding or an already committed action. Melee can use legal spare capacity around the screen; no support teleports aside and no free intra-hex reshuffling is introduced.

Alternative: deterministic withdrawal would require extra retreat actions and timing/priority rules when transfer arrives. Permanent deployment affinity makes the obstruction impossible, applies equally to both factions and has a simple bounded admission proof. Permanently assigned combat lanes would change the requested closest-target rule; fill-one-hex-first would make splash/congestion dominate automatic placement.

### 2. Keep action, occupancy and dying state in the existing authority-owned ECS

Replace continuous body components with location/footprint, lifecycle and action components. A live unit is Queued, Ready/Waiting, Moving, Windup or Recovery. Dying is a terminal lifecycle state retaining its identity until cleanup. Common action metadata includes a monotonically increasing sequence and start/end ticks; move state additionally identifies both footprints, route/transit identity and arrival tick; attack state identifies the locked primary, impact and ready ticks. Record death start/end and frozen movement progress when applicable.

The battlefield owns position/transit reservations keyed by unit/action identity and an occupancy revision. Reserve source, destination and transit conflicts in one transaction; failed requests make no partial changes. Derive occupancy from ECS actions during consistency checks/reconstruction so an auxiliary reservation index cannot silently become a conflicting unit store. Living soldier/enemy DTO projections filter out Dying; snapshots expose a separate current dying-body collection. Existing survivor/allocation queries must not count retained corpses.

Use checked integer health/tick arithmetic and bounded stable-ID counters. Timers are absolute ticks, not decrementing floats. New waves invalidate previous routes/actions, preserve surviving identities/health, and reform only after prior death reservations finish. Teardown disposes all units, reservation indexes, caches and events through the existing `Match`/`AuthoritySession` lifetime.

Alternative: remove ECS casualties and retain a presentation-only corpse, as today, cannot implement authoritative capacity release or restore it after a history gap.

### 3. Specify action scheduling and simultaneous impacts

Refactor `CombatSimulation.Step`/`Match.Step` around explicit phases:

| Phase | Authoritative effect |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Complete due intervals | Commit move arrivals, finish recovery, remove expired deaths and release reservations |
| 2. Resolve due impacts | Read one common alive/location state, validate unit/defense primaries, accumulate all damage |
| 3. Apply casualties | Apply accumulated unit/city health changes; enter Dying and cancel future living actions |
| 4. Reconcile cities/entries | Eliminate all zero-health cities together, transfer living attackers, then admit queued entries |
| 5. Resolve progression | Apply ordinary health defeat/wave completion, otherwise evaluate fight limits |
| 6. Start actions | Gather ready decisions from a common snapshot; arbitrate compatible moves; start attacks/moves |
| 7. Publish | Emit canonical events/current state and trim bounded history |

At an arrival/impact coincidence, phase 1's destination is used. At a recovery/lethal-impact coincidence, phase 3 prevents a new action. Same-tick lethal impacts remain simultaneous; initiative never removes the second valid hit. Due city impacts additionally validate that the city is still exposed to that attacker and belongs to its current destination. An arriving defending soldier invalidates exposure on subsequent impacts, not retroactively before phase 4 admission.

All attacks have positive windup and recovery, so phase 6 cannot also create a phase 2 impact that tick. Attack cadence is windup plus recovery. Hold position through both phases, including when the primary dies early. Hit poses are cosmetic layers and do not reset timers. Every impact/miss has an action identity and consumes normal recovery; splash primary validation precedes victim selection.

Gather intents without letting earlier decisions mutate the target-selection snapshot. Arbitrate moves in ascending initiative, then persistent seeded scheduling rank, with identity as collision fallback. Validate each against already accepted reservations. Independent actions start on the same tick. Deterministic event order uses tick/phase and stable actor/action/victim identity; random scheduling does not imply random wire traversal.

Alternative: resolve a complete unit turn before the next unit would introduce traversal-dependent damage and destroy simultaneous lethal strikes. Delaying each later actor by a render frame would make initiative depend on presentation.

### 4. Define target ranking separately from route planning

At each ready decision, first enumerate in-range living deployed opponents in the same city. Rank by graph hex distance, target initiative, then a seeded target choice. Ranged/magic ranges include distance one. Pick a fresh primary at each new attack; do not retarget a committed windup. Allies do not block projectiles and no line-of-sight rule is added.

With no in-range opponent, query routes to free legal attack footprints: cells at distance 1..Range from each eligible target, with compatible faction/capacity/deployment affinity. Rank opponents by their minimum current feasible route length, then target initiative and a seeded target tie. Only then apply the formation goal preferences to equal-distance goal footprints and choose remaining equal goals/routes using separate random purposes; an enemy with many possible approach positions must not receive extra probability in the primary tie.

Use cached static distances plus deterministic breadth-first searches over footprint-compatible cells. Current occupied/reserved positions constrain feasibility. If no current route exists, keep an objective ranked by static approach distance and wait for relevant capacity changes; temporary reservations are not proof that an enemy is permanently unreachable. This distinction allows queues behind fighting allies rather than redirecting continuously. Enemies with no living deployed defender instead approach attack positions for the virtual city anchor; allies with no deployed target wait for admission/transfers.

Routes belong to a pursuit episode. Follow a simple route one committed step at a time, reducing its remaining step count. Refresh it when the target's attackable hex changes, the objective becomes invalid or the next step is blocked by changed occupancy. Preserve the objective and tie choices during unchanged congestion; prevent returning to a visited cell within an unchanged route episode. The finite fight limit handles moving-objective cycles that local routing alone cannot prove impossible.

A waiting unit records relevant board/target revisions and a retry deadline (initially six ticks). Important changes wake it sooner; repeated checks of unchanged state do not reroll. Cache routing by board revision, faction, range and footprint class, and bound searches to the finite board. Rendering never triggers a numerical search. Advancing a visual frame does not increment decision sequences.

Alternative: always chase smallest direct distance ignores blocked access. A full space-time global path optimizer adds complexity and cost unnecessary for this small board. Per-tick target rerolls and local lateral steering cause thrashing.

### 5. Reserve movement routes and keep one attackable location

For an accepted one-hex move, retain the full source footprint and reserve the full destination footprint. The attackable hex remains the source throughout the interval and switches at arrival. The destination reservation also establishes faction ownership before visual travel begins. There is no intra-hex attack or free intra-hex rearrangement action in this version.

Declare route families between footprint anchors and boundary portals. They use fixed transit conflict identifiers, not collision testing. Initially serialize all movement touching the same hex using an endpoint-hex transit token plus an undirected shared-edge token. This conservative rule prevents moving allies from crossing inside a shared hex and forbids opposing swaps, while unused stationary capacity remains shareable. Authored routes must pass around fixed occupied anchors; confirm clearance using bundled model scale in graphical evidence. Keep route/conflict metadata with the versioned layout and refuse missing transitions before combat. More concurrent transit lanes can be added later without changing the reservation contract.

At death during transit, freeze route progress from the authority tick and retain both endpoints and transit tokens until death end. The headless authority records only route identity and integer elapsed/duration; the client derives its frozen model location. This avoids snapping a casualty back to its source or allowing another model through it. On transfer of a living mover, cancel its route and release all former-city tokens immediately; it becomes queued at its new entrance, preserving any preexisting attack recovery.

Alternative: lock the entire hex's capacity during every move would prevent sharing unused positions. Endpoint positions alone do not prevent route crossings. A halfway attackable-location switch adds another meaningful action boundary without a need in the initial model.

### 6. Freeze and validate a central combat configuration

Add immutable combat profiles/configuration to the existing `Rules`/catalog boundary rather than installing a new config package. Authority construction validates and copies the data; snapshots expose effective catalogs and a canonical board/configuration fingerprint. Centralize specialist, tower and defender durations currently hardcoded in `Catalogs`. All unit range and splash comparisons use integer graph distance. Health/damage research remains integer hundredths, affecting only those two stats.

Initial tuning candidates at 60 Hz:

| Type | Capacity | Initiative | Move ticks | Range | Windup | Recovery | Death ticks |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Swordsman | 2 | 10 | 30 | 1 | 12 | 48 | 48 |
| Berserker | 2 | 20 | 27 | 1 | 18 | 54 | 48 |
| Crossbowman | 1 | 30 | 30 | 3 | 18 | 42 | 48 |
| Mage | 1 | 40 | 30 | 3 | 24 | 66 | 48 |

These preserve current unit attack start-to-start intervals but intentionally change spatial behavior; retained HP/damage is not a constraint that can defeat role acceptance. Candidate Mage damage rises from 2 to 4 while its health stays 6, cadence 90 ticks, range three, capacity one and recruitment cost seven food/two gold. Crossbowman remains damage 3, health 8, cadence 60 and cost five food. At full sustained cadence this is Mage 2.67 damage/second for one victim or 5.33 for two, versus Crossbowman 3 single-target. The old Mage two-victim rate of 2.67 was below Crossbowman; it is explicitly rejected as a sufficient clustered role baseline. Both factions use the same revised Mage profile. These are gate candidates, not a claim of proven balance. Mage/Catapult start with hex splash radius zero and cap three so clustered occupants, not adjacent entire formations, receive the initial splash. Arrow and built-in defender start with windup 12/recovery 48; Catapult uses 30/90. Defender damage and tower level damage remain existing catalog values. Tower targeting uses the common defense anchor with unlimited access to deployed enemies in its city; unit city attacks use the unit's own range. Defense exact ties have their own action keys.

Initial limits are 3,600 no-health-progress ticks per active city engagement and 18,000 maximum wave combat ticks. The no-progress allowance must exceed conservative initial approach, reservation/entry throughput, death and longest attack delays for the configured board/formation; reject a clearly shorter allowance. The early strategy/role/crowd and rendered gate determines whether these values and board spacing are viable before broad integration; final tuning repeats only checks affected by later changes. Record final values/fingerprint and seeds in verification rather than claiming these candidates are already balanced. Changing values is permitted within these contracts before implementation completion; retain all required winning/losing strategy families.

Reject nonpositive durations, negative ranges/initiative/capacity, footprint costs with no legal entry placement, invalid masks/transitions, missing/duplicate cells, asymmetric neighbors, disconnected required approaches, protected entries without compatible footprints/frontier access, virtual-anchor links that require entering opponent-protected cells, nonpositive limits, and overflow-prone tick/health values. Keep fixed 60 Hz as the declared simulation rate; duration fields are configurable. No user settings or mutable runtime config changes the fight.

Alternative: a separate maximum count per type is ambiguous for mixed units. Floating speeds converted repeatedly to step durations obscure exact boundaries. A config-file/UI project is unnecessary for centralized tweakable rules.

### 7. Make combat randomness explicit and reproducible

Accept an explicit non-secret 64-bit combat seed through numerical match construction/fixtures. When omitted, the authority generates it once using its existing platform randomness and retains it in complete state; generation never shares a stream with credentials or cosmetics. Derive wave/city domains using stable numeric identifiers, not match GUID or process hashes. Canonical setup includes stable unit identities, roster/wave inputs, unit profiles/health, placements or formation policy, defense state and ordered accepted gameplay inputs. Connection metadata may change whole snapshots but cannot change the normalized combat trace unless it changes accepted gameplay inputs.

Use a versioned, explicitly implemented 64-bit SplitMix-style mixer/local stream with unsigned unchecked wraparound, fixed shift/constants, fixed numeric purpose tags and canonical numeric-field order. Pin algorithm outputs with golden vectors; never use `System.Random`, `GetHashCode` or dictionary order as the combat algorithm. A decision key includes seed/rules version, wave, city, actor or action identity, logical decision sequence and purpose. Local streams use rejection sampling for unbiased selection from canonically sorted tied candidates.

For algorithm version one, define `Mix(x)` by XOR-shift 30 then multiply by `0xBF58476D1CE4E5B9`, XOR-shift 27 then multiply by `0x94D049BB133111EB`, then XOR-shift 31, all on unsigned 64-bit values. Start key state at the master seed and fold each fixed-width numeric field as `Mix(state + 0x9E3779B97F4A7C15 + field)` with wraparound. Use the fixed tuple order algorithm version, configuration fingerprint words, wave, city, actor kind, actor identity, action/decision sequence, reevaluation generation, purpose. Configuration fingerprint words use canonical byte ordering, never runtime object hashes. A local stream advances state by `0x9E3779B97F4A7C15` and returns `Mix(state)`. Purpose tags one through six mean target, movement rank, goal footprint, route, formation and splash respectively. Persist any encoding revision as a new algorithm version, and pin both mixer and composed decision-key golden vectors.

For contested movement, compute/store a 64-bit scheduling rank per actor's current ready decision. Sort equal initiative by that rank with identity collision fallback. A blocked actor retains its rank/target/route tie decisions until a relevant decision change; successful action completion creates the next decision sequence. Targets, route/goal choices, formation placement and splash ties use distinct purpose tags so added visual randomness or a splash tie cannot consume another unit's choices. Explicitly encode any legitimate reevaluation generation in its decision state; repeating an unchanged wait does not create one.

Persist sequences/ranks and current objective state in authoritative components and snapshots needed for full numerical reconstruction. Clients use resolved authoritative actions; they do not run a random combat stream. Replay tests compare normalized sorted combat state/events, including deaths/reservations/decision state, not GUIDs, connection fields or unrelated revision bookkeeping. Print the first divergent tick with seed/fingerprint when they disagree.

Alternative: a single seeded global PRNG can be deterministic only if every consumer's call count/order remains perfectly fixed. Per-decision streams keep tie purposes independent and make unchanged retries auditable.

### 8. End stalled fights deterministically without fabricating health changes

Track a no-progress deadline for each city with living enemies assigned, including queued enemies, and a maximum duration from wave combat start. Starting a genuinely new engagement initializes its allowance. While an engagement remains active, only actual unit or city health reduction in that city resets its deadline. Movement, admissions, misses, zero-damage hits, target rerolls and repeated transfers do not reset it. A cleared city's deadline is inactive; new incoming enemies activate it with an initial allowance, while the global wave limit prevents repeated clear/reactivate episodes extending combat indefinitely.

Evaluate normal all-cities-fallen defeat and enemy-free wave completion before limits after due damage/transfers/admission. If a limit expires while living enemies remain, choose a canonical diagnostic (lowest triggered city ID, with no-progress before wave-duration when both trigger), stop new combat actions and publish `Defeat` with `BattleStalled`, limit kind, city when applicable and tick. Preserve current health and do not call city elimination or redistribution merely to manufacture the result. Normal defeat uses `AllCitiesFallen`. This matches the user's explicit selected stalled outcome and avoids adding a fourth terminal phase.

Freeze active living action presentation at the terminal tick and cancel future impacts. Record interrupted movement progress so the final view does not jump to a different anchor. Existing dying bodies still finish their bounded cleanup. Deadline diagnostics additionally retain non-secret seed/configuration, targets, current routes, reservations and last health-progress tick for investigation.

Alternative: a global health-progress timer lets another city's fighting hide a deadlock. Counting movement as progress permits endless circling. Removing a victim or granting damage to break a stalemate would corrupt the stated combat rules.

### 9. Run authoritative death cleanup beyond the combat phase

Keep one monotonic authority action tick. `Match.Step` advances it during Combat and whenever unexpired dying bodies need cleanup in Building/Victory/Defeat, provided shared pause is false. Outside Combat only death cleanup and a pending ordinary ready-check resolution run; there is no movement, damage, production or fight-limit evaluation. Publish changed cleanup state through the ordinary snapshot channel at its bounded cadence. A frozen paused match is still byte-stable apart from normal permitted connection/command revisions.

Wave completion is immediate for gameplay counts/results: Dying does not keep the enemy allocation alive. Building may be editable while cleanup runs. If rapid readiness reaches the next preparation barrier first, retain readiness and automatically start once cleanup is empty and normal eligibility holds. Increment production/stage guards only for their existing legitimate transitions; cleanup grants no income. Teardown/session replacement releases retained state immediately because that battlefield is no longer reused.

`CombatPlayback` follows this authority tick through cleanup rather than timing death with `VisualSeconds`. Initial/baseline/gap acceptance reconstructs current alive and Dying DTOs. Do not replay historical effects. Pending death state supplies its frozen route pose and elapsed animation; expired bodies are absent. Pause or transport loss freezes the sampled tick; reconnect seeks to the synchronized tick. Map the existing imported death clip to configured duration just as attack markers map to impact/recovery. Ambient presentation may retain its independent cosmetic seconds.

Alternative: waiting for a client's animation callback cannot work for headless or disconnected roles. Holding a wave in Combat until the final corpse expires would change ordinary victory/count semantics. A second unsynchronized death clock risks drifting occupancy and visual expiry.

### 10. Version the wire contract and map rendering from authoritative cells

Extend complete snapshots with seed/rules/board identity and sufficient declared board/configuration data, live action/footprint state, current dying bodies/reservations and outcome reason. Extend events with authoritative action/death intervals and hex/position impact identity; movement starts/completions and reservation releases must be attributable by action even if some are represented only in current state. Bounded event history remains cosmetic delivery, never the only source of occupancy. Bump `WireJson.ProtocolVersion` from the current value; older peers are refused by the existing handshake. Update tests/docs that currently disagree with the source version. Credentials and transport lifecycle retain their current protection.

Replace `BattlePoint`/continuous collision rules and their DTO consumers as one coordinated change. Do not retain `Position/Lateral` as a second numerical truth. If temporary compatibility fields are needed during implementation, derive them only from declared anchors/action progress and remove them before final integration. `CombatPlayback`, `UnitView`, health bars, defender/tower effects and DevRunner observations consume the same mapping. Godot world vectors/trigonometry are allowed for rendering/camera projection; numerical combat only uses graph/position/action identities.

Map cells to the existing medieval tile lattice, using distinct fixed anchors per footprint and declared transit routes at the actual support surface. Keep decorations out of this patch. Supply full combat bounds to the final camera/countryside interfaces rather than assuming the old narrow lane. Preserve imported rigs, required clips, health bars, local camera navigation, plot selection and asset provenance. Menu landscape can show the same clear patch without creating an authority or units. The HUD displays `Battle stalled` instead of `all cities fell` for the new reason; no seed editor is added.

Alternative: client-derived occupancy or historical death nodes cannot restore current reservations. Silently accepting older DTOs risks absent defaults creating invalid board/action state.

### 10.1. Deliberately depict abstract tabletop melee and prove it early

Choose abstract tabletop combat. At roughly three world units between neighboring terrain centers, a stationary miniature cannot physically reach every far-side occupant with the existing sword/axe clip. The visual contract therefore includes an explicit short directional slash/strike connection from the attacking miniature to the locked target and target-side impact/hit response at the authoritative tick. Preserve the actual rigged swing and facing at its footprint anchor. Show attempts/misses without a landed-target flash. Ordinary generic sparks with no attacker/target relationship do not satisfy this contract. Effects derive from action identity and the shared clock and do not apply damage, move combat locations or consume combat randomness.

The cue must read as one tabletop melee action; it is not a ballistic projectile or a claim of blade-to-body contact. Do not stretch weapons, slide models between anchors during attacks or silently change board scale to imply contact. Subtle action-linked direction and impact feedback makes the abstraction visible, including a target on the far side of the adjacent hex. Source-until-arrival remains a numerical rule; a hit cue follows the target's actual sampled model position without changing its attackable hex.

Before accepting the tile spacing/footprint layout, run a short checked-in early proof in the existing source combat slice: two occupied adjacent cells, actual bundled melee rigs, near-side and far-side target anchors, more than one occupant in a cell and at least one simultaneous exchange. Reach the engagement using ordinary setup/recruitment/progression, then pause at action milestones using the existing ordered driver. An early sub-selection of this slice may stop after its owned setup/assertions/captures; it creates no new full-match scenario, test-only combat setter or external SceneTree probe. Capture windup and impact frames and inspect target attribution and readable role/health bars at the supported overview and a close view. Protocol assertions and non-overlapping coordinates are necessary but do not establish visual readability.

Implement only the minimal ordinary snapshot projection/protocol bump and source renderer/observations needed for that check before the gate. Defer comprehensive reconnect/history/package/feedback integration until it passes. If ordinary placement never creates the requested near/far shared-cell engagement, that is a formation-proof failure to fix at this gate, not permission to seed a special graphical layout. Missing evidence or an unconvincing empty-air swing with unlinked sparks leaves the gate open. Correct cue/anchor/scale candidates and rerun the affected proof before continuing.

Alternative: believable physical contact would require reserved presentation approach/lunge positions and simultaneous multi-attacker choreography, or a much smaller battlefield representation. Those change the fixed-anchor design substantially. The explicit abstraction provides a coherent initial presentation contract with the existing assets; changing to contact choreography is a new design decision, not an implementation fallback.

### 11. Extend existing verification by the defect each layer can observe

Cheap core tests own graph distance/route correctness, mixed footprints/fragmentation, faction exclusivity, atomic conflict admission, source-until-arrival targeting, death during movement, queue conservation, simultaneous strikes, impact misses, city exposure, splash order/caps, pause, transfer recovery, cleanup barriers, exact limit ticks, validation and disposal. Use explicit seeds in all existing equivalence fixtures. Pin random golden vectors and alternate-seed ties; compare full normalized traces under reversed insertion, unrelated GUIDs and repeated unchanged decisions. Run fixed-seed small-board congestion cases and the existing 32-vs-32/mixed and 80-entry queue cases, checking every tick's invariants and ordinary completion without using the stalled limit as a successful crowd result. Existing zero-damage timing fixtures may need an explicit sufficiently long limit so they test timing rather than prematurely terminate.

Run the gameplay gate as soon as minimal board, occupancy, seeded actions, admission, deaths and normal three-wave progression work: before comprehensive wire/reconnect/feedback/package integration. Occupancy/reservation primitives precede route/target consumers in the task order. Preserve `VillageStrategyTests` families and one-through-four-player winning strategy. Re-evaluate their finite ordinary commands across a checked-in small seed set, recording seeds, costs, losses, health and fight ticks; tune default profile/layout values when necessary. A seed sample is evidence for those strategies, not universal balance or deadlock freedom. Add three small paired core role fixtures using normal profiles and the declared formation policy: (1) a live nearer melee screen protects support through its initial attacks, with an otherwise identical unscreened control showing earlier support damage; (2) a crowded mixed formation admits a queued melee unit and lets it reach an attack while opponents remain, without moving/replacing a support unit for free; (3) a fixed ordinary clustered setup supplies at least two actual splash victims, and replacing one Crossbowman with one Mage provides greater effective, non-overkill support damage over a shared live observation interval plus an improvement in clear time or surviving friendly/city health. Also compare an isolated single-target control in which Crossbowman retains superior sustained damage per support unit. Log actual recruitment gold/food, health and action/victim counts; this one-unit role comparison is not an equal-cost army or universal efficiency claim. A mixed win with no effective Mage secondary damage fails Mage acceptance. Normal formation must produce the cluster; fabricated dense positions, inflated target HP or maximum-cap-only victim geometry do not count.

Extend existing network `authority-resume-victory` and `redistribution` state assertions for seed/configuration equality, moving/death state, current reservation validity and transfer cleanup. Include transfer into a previously cleared city with surviving defenders on neutral enemy-forward cells: assert actual protected-row admission within its release bound and subsequent engagement, not just conserved allocations. Retain ordinary all-cities-fallen expectations in the no-investment `defeat` case; a stalled defeat must not make that strategy test pass. Current sorted DTO/protocol assertions can observe these changes without new processes. No new full network scenario is needed.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Conservative transit locking slows queues] -> Measure default/crowded throughput and tune finite board/move durations first; only add declared compatible transit lanes if observed cost warrants it.
- [Capacity footprints fragment cells] -> Test mixed and larger synthetic profiles against actual legal masks; expose why admission waits and reject unusable profiles.
- [New spatial scale/radius changes balance] -> Preserve numerical research and required strategy families, evaluate fixed seed samples and record final tuning rather than weakening outcome assertions.
- [Target/route changes still form pursuit cycles] -> Stable pursuit decisions, revision-based waits, per-city health deadlines and a global duration bound make the failure explicit and attributable. Protected deployment prevents the known cleared-board reinforcement obstruction; ordinary admission cannot pass by timing out.
- [Abstract melee still reads as an empty-air swing] -> Gate actual near/far occupied-cell attacks with linked strike/impact cues and inspect rendered evidence before fixing layout scale or integrating packages.
- [Formation hides a weak or redundant Mage] -> Specify frontage/rear/fallback rules, require paired effective contribution and single-target controls, and tune both faction profiles before broad integration.
- [Corpse/cleanup state is counted as a living enemy] -> Separate living projections from retained Dying DTOs and test victory, redistribution and rapid next-wave readiness.

- [Seed claims exceed supported compatibility] -> Version the algorithm/configuration, pin golden vectors and compare supported runtimes; cross-version replay remains outside scope.
